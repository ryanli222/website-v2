## Overall Impression

The portfolio is calm, coherent, and technically credible. Its strongest asset is authentic work, not decoration. The largest opportunity is editorial: make the hierarchy, interaction cues, and final professional proposition as deliberate as the projects themselves.

## What's Working

- The homepage's biography/artifact split lets personality and technical proof coexist without a conventional oversized hero.
- Real images and substantive projects create strong technical texture across assistive robotics, Formula Electric, embedded systems, and tooling.
- The component system is restrained and consistent; shared card behavior, typography, spacing, and image containment create cohesion without detector-flagged visual gimmicks.

## Priority Issues

### P1 — The primary CV link is broken

**Why it matters:** Recruiters are explicitly invited to open the CV, then encounter a 404 at a high-trust moment.

**Fix:** Link `cv` directly to an existing PDF or implement `/resume`, then add automated checking for global navigation destinations.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable harden`

### P1 — Contrast, focus, and motion are visually fragile

**Why it matters:** Small `#999` text, pale hero copy, and subtle icons are likely below WCAG AA against white. There is no clear focus-visible system or reduced-motion treatment, while delayed opacity reveals can postpone or hide content.

**Fix:** Raise muted-text contrast, add an obvious keyboard focus treatment, and collapse animation delays under `prefers-reduced-motion`.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable audit`

### P1 — The mobile header is likely to crowd or overflow

**Why it matters:** Six links share one non-wrapping row with fixed gaps and generous page padding, leaving little room on narrow phones.

**Fix:** Test at 320–390px and introduce a deliberate mobile pattern: compact secondary external links, allow a second row, or use an accessible overflow menu while keeping Projects and Experience visible.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable adapt`

### P2 — Inert hero words advertise interaction

**Why it matters:** Pointer cursors and animated underlines teach visitors that highlighted words are clickable; doing nothing on activation damages predictability.

**Fix:** Remove the interaction styling or convert those words into real links to relevant portfolio sections.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

### P2 — Index pages need editorial hierarchy

**Why it matters:** Equal-weight cards make visitors decide among six projects without guidance, while Experience presents a very sparse Hestus entry with the same visual importance as detailed UWFE work.

**Fix:** Add concise route headings or orientation copy, identify a recommended starting point, and differentiate summary impact from metadata without adding clutter.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable layout`

## Cognitive Load

Overall cognitive load is moderate, with two checklist failures:

- **Visual hierarchy:** Projects and Experience begin directly with equal-weight cards and no route-level heading, summary, or featured starting point.
- **Minimal choices:** Projects presents six visually equivalent options, exceeding the four-item working-memory target without grouping.

Single focus, chunking, grouping, one-thing-at-a-time flow, working-memory support, and progressive disclosure otherwise pass.

## Emotional Journey

The entry is warm and human, and the Autonomous Wheelchair/YHack win creates a strong early credibility peak. The middle becomes emotionally flat as pale cards repeat with similar weight. Experience is thinner than Projects; the deliberately concise “Datasets and tooling.” copy can read as evasive beside UWFE's detail. The footer is pleasant but does not create a strong final professional beat or contact invitation, so the portfolio's peak occurs early rather than at the end.

- Underlined hero words invite literal clicking but do nothing.
- “Datasets and tooling.” does not explain professional value.
- The shared arrow does not reveal whether a card opens a case study or leaves the site.

### Riley — Deliberate stress tester

- Delayed opacity reveals have no visible reduced-motion fallback.

### Casey — Distracted mobile user

- Fixed card media heights create a long comparison scroll.
- The 1.2–2.15 second reveal sequence delays useful content for impatient or slow-connection visitors.

## Minor Observations

- Project titles function as 13px metadata rather than strong scanning anchors.
- The homepage and index routes omit semantic H1 headings.
- Experience image alt text repeats the role instead of describing the image.
- External navigation deserves a visible or accessible cue distinct from internal cards.
- “Built by Ryan” spends the final interaction opportunity on authorship already obvious from the site.
